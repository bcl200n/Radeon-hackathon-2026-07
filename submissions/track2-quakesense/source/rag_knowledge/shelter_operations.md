# Shelter capacity and admission management

1. A shelter approaching its capacity limit should begin signalling reduced admission before it is completely full, so arriving groups can be redirected while they still have time and route options left, rather than discovering the shelter is full only upon arrival.
2. Reopening admission after a period of throttling should require a genuine, sustained margin of free capacity, not a single moment of measured headroom, since occupancy near a threshold can fluctuate and a shelter that flips between "admitting" and "full" repeatedly causes more disruption (repeated rerouting of approaching groups) than a shelter that stays firmly in one state.
3. When a shelter fills and closes to new arrivals, people already re-routed toward it need a specific, reachable alternative communicated promptly — a rejection without a next destination is worse than no update at all.
4. Track and report shelter capacity by category where relevant (general population, medical needs, accessibility needs) rather than a single aggregate number, since a shelter can be "full" for general capacity while still able to admit someone with an urgent medical need through a separate arrangement.

# Site selection principles

1. New or additional shelter sites are most valuable when placed to cover population that is currently unserved (no reachable shelter within an acceptable distance/capacity), not simply to add capacity near already-well-served areas.
2. Each additional site placed should be evaluated against the marginal population it newly serves, not the total population near it, since much of that population may already have a closer or equally good option.
3. Candidate site data (a location appearing in a public map or historical list) is not the same as a confirmed, resourced, currently-operational shelter; treat unverified candidates as provisional until an authority confirms capacity, access and services.
