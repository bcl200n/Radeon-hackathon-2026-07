# Shelter selection and information verification

1. Compare walking reachability, current road hazards, remaining shelter capacity and personal mobility needs before choosing a destination. The nearest shelter is not always the safest reachable shelter if its access route is compromised or it is already near capacity.
2. Record the issue time and source of each piece of route or shelter-status information you act on. Cross-check unusual or urgent claims from a single unofficial source against at least one other channel before changing a plan based on them.
3. If a target shelter reports full, select the next validated reachable option rather than waiting at a full shelter's entrance, and communicate the capacity change back to anyone following the same route.
4. Prefer a designated or officially confirmed shelter over an unverified candidate location when both are reachable in similar time, since capacity and services at unverified locations are not guaranteed.
5. When a route choice is close between two options, prefer the one with lower reported congestion and no reported blockage over the one that is marginally shorter, since congestion and blockage risk compound over the walk rather than resolving on their own.
6. Re-plan periodically rather than committing permanently to an initial route: road status, shelter capacity and hazard extent all change during an evacuation, and information that was accurate at departure may not remain accurate 20-30 minutes later.

# Evacuation phasing

1. Immediate phase (0-10 minutes): prioritize personal safety, a basic structural check of the immediate surroundings, and beginning movement toward a safe assembly point, not a final shelter, if the final destination is not yet clear.
2. Movement phase (10-60 minutes): follow the best currently available route to a reachable shelter, adjusting for new blockage or capacity information as it arrives.
3. Consolidation phase (60+ minutes): once at a shelter, register presence if a system exists to do so, and avoid premature return trips to a damaged area to retrieve belongings until officials confirm it is safe.
