# Community emergency response

1. Community coordinators should verify the source before relaying instructions, then send concise and consistent messages through multiple channels so the same guidance reaches people who only monitor one of them.
2. Continue reporting route access, shelter capacity and medical needs upward to whatever coordination point exists, since these change quickly and downstream decisions depend on current, not initial, status.
3. Mark unverified road-closure or secondary-hazard reports as pending verification, and do not use a single unverified report as the sole basis for a network-wide routing decision; a false blockage report that gets acted on can be as disruptive as a real one.
4. Emergency responders prioritize immediate threats to life (trapped people, active fire, structural collapse in progress) over property protection; medical workers triage critical, serious and minor injuries in that order rather than first-come-first-served.
5. Where volunteers or untrained community members are assisting, keep their role to reporting, guiding and basic first aid; defer structural entry, technical rescue and advanced medical care to trained responders even under time pressure.

# Rumor and misinformation handling

1. Treat an unusually urgent or alarming claim (e.g. a specific shelter is unsafe, a specific route is impassable) with extra scrutiny precisely because urgency itself is the mechanism that suppresses verification.
2. Prefer official or multiply-corroborated sources for any claim that would cause many people to change route simultaneously, since a wrong claim at that scale creates its own congestion and risk.
3. When a claim cannot be verified in the time available, propagate it as "unverified: acted on with caution" rather than either suppressing it entirely or treating it as confirmed.
