# Basic post-earthquake safety principles

1. After shaking stops, check for falling objects, damaged power lines, gas leaks and visible structural damage before moving. Do not run blindly out of a building while it is still shaking.
2. Follow reliable on-site guidance and walk to an open, reachable shelter with available capacity. Do not use elevators; use stairs, and avoid stairwells with visible cracking or debris.
3. If a route is blocked, burning or dangerously congested, stop before entering it, return to a safe node and replan rather than pushing through.
4. Assist older people, children, injured people and people with limited mobility without entering an unevaluated damaged building to do so; call for trained responders if a rescue requires entering unstable structures.
5. Expect aftershocks for hours to days after a significant earthquake. Aftershocks can be strong enough to complete a partial collapse a first shock only started, so avoid re-entering a visibly damaged building even to retrieve belongings.
6. If trapped, protect your airway from dust, tap on a pipe or wall in bursts of three to signal location instead of shouting continuously (shouting uses air and energy), and stay still to avoid disturbing loose debris.
7. Outdoors, move away from building facades, glass, signage and power lines, since falling debris and downed lines remain a hazard well after the main shock.
8. If near the coast and the earthquake was strong or long, treat a tsunami warning as a life-safety instruction to move to high ground immediately, without waiting for official confirmation if natural warning signs (unusual sea recession, rumbling) are present.
