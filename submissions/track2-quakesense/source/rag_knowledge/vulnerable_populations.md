# Assisting vulnerable populations during evacuation

1. Older adults and people with mobility impairments may need more time to reach a shelter than a route's estimated walking time assumes; route and capacity planning that uses a single population-wide speed will systematically under-serve this group unless a slower-mobility allowance is built in.
2. Children separated from a guardian during a chaotic evacuation should be moved to a supervised, marked assembly point rather than left to make an independent routing decision; reunification information should be logged (who, where found, where taken) as early as possible.
3. People with medical dependencies (dialysis, insulin, oxygen, mobility equipment charging) may need to prioritize a shelter with the relevant capability over the nearest or least congested one; capture this need explicitly rather than assuming any reachable shelter is equally suitable.
4. Language and communication barriers can mean official guidance does not reach everyone equally; community relay through multiple channels and formats reduces this gap more than a single broadcast channel does.
5. People without independent transportation or mobility have fewer alternative routes if their first choice is blocked; this population benefits disproportionately from proactive rerouting rather than expecting them to independently discover and act on a route change.

This is synthetic educational knowledge for the QuakeScope AI research prototype. It is not an official emergency plan and does not replace local authority guidance.
